> **Timeline alignment:** The [public AI Product Marketing resume](https://github.com/beastofbayarea/shivam-singh-ai-pmm/blob/main/shivam-singh-ai-pmm.pdf) is used only to place this case within the McKinsey role dated July 2014-June 2016.

## Evidence-grounded premise

McKinsey's 2015 retail-pricing research describes elasticity, price perception, item segmentation, guardrails, and rapid test-and-learn as core capabilities. The World Bank's impact-evaluation handbook explains counterfactual reasoning and causal methods. These sources support commercializing pricing analytics as a governed decision product, not a black-box recommendation engine.

## Case approach

- Segment items by customer value perception, demand response, competition, economics, and category role.
- Estimate elasticity and validate recommendations with comparison groups or controlled experiments where feasible.
- Expose assumptions, guardrails, confidence, and expected trade-offs to merchants.
- Position the product around measurable decision quality, explainability, and safe adoption.

## Evidence-based success measures

Use experiment validity, forecast error, guardrail breaches, merchant adoption, override reasons, margin, volume, and price-perception indicators. These are proposed measures, not reported historical results.
