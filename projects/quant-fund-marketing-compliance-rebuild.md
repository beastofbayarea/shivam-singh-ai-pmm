# Quant Fund Marketing — Compliance Rebuild and Channel Quality

I completed this work during my [D. E. Shaw experience from July 2016 to December 2019](https://github.com/beastofbayarea/shivam-singh-ai-pmm/blob/main/shivam-singh-ai-pmm.pdf).

## The incident

A high-volume campaign created weak meeting conversion while an unsupported performance claim put a $50M prospect pipeline at risk. An audit found related exposure in roughly 25% of more than 1,000 assets. The problem was not one sentence; it was a production system in which Marketing, Legal, and Compliance worked sequentially and broad channels rewarded attention rather than investor relevance.

I chose a controlled three-month pause. Patching individual assets would have preserved the same incentives, handoffs, and evidence gaps. The pause created space to rebuild the communications system before restarting demand generation.

## The rule I used to redesign the system

Every material claim had to be useful to the investor and provable to the regulator.

FINRA Rule 2210 made the standard concrete: communications must be fair and balanced, cannot contain false or exaggerated claims, need appropriate approval and recordkeeping, and cannot imply that past performance will recur. COSO’s internal-control framework helped me turn those obligations into ownership, information, monitoring, and corrective action.

## Claim-level substantiation

I linked each material claim to approved evidence, scope, date, version history, required qualification, and Marketing–Legal–Compliance signatures. That moved the unit of governance from the finished asset to the claim itself.

I also shifted risk detection into drafting. Writers received approved evidence-backed modules and real-time prompts, allowing them to resolve common issues before final review. More than 200 commercial staff were trained on the new workflow, and they resolved 80% of flagged issues without a late legal rewrite.

Immutable approvals and source history made it possible to answer who approved a claim, which evidence supported it, where it appeared, and whether the evidence remained current.

## Rebuilding channel quality

The compliance failure exposed a second commercial problem: the campaign optimized traffic and form fills rather than qualified institutional interest. I shifted investment toward higher-intent investor channels and process-led content that explained discipline, controls, and risk instead of implying superior returns.

I measured qualified meetings, resumed conversations, and commitments—not raw lead volume. This aligned the channel strategy with the same credibility standard as the content.

## The outcome

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---:|
| Paused conversations resumed | 90% |
| Prospect pipeline restored | $50M |
| New AUM | $12M |
| Review time | -40% |
| Annual legal-review cost | -$500K |
| Enforcement penalties | None |

The absence of an enforcement fine mattered, but the deeper result was operating. The company could produce useful investor communications faster because evidence and responsibility were designed into the workflow.

## Attribution boundary

I report the program outcomes I directly measured. I do not extend them into claims about fund performance or guaranteed investment returns.
